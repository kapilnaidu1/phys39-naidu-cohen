Phys 39 Module 2, Part 3 records.
Trim pot -> PWM -> BTS7960 H-bridge -> DC motor.
Naidu / Cohen

Sketch: arduino/module_02/trimpot_hbridge/trimpot_hbridge.ino, maxDuty = 255.
Instrument for Part 3B/3C signal check: BK Precision 2120B, dual trace,
30 MHz analog oscilloscope. TEC module and thermal switch DISCONNECTED
for the whole of Part 3; the load terminals drove the small DC motor only.


1. PWM TIMEBASE (computed, not measured)

    Pins 9 and 10 are both on Timer1. The Arduino core runs Timer1 in
    phase-correct mode with prescaler 64, so

        f = 16 MHz / (64 * 510) = 490.196 Hz
        T = 2040.0 us

    and one analogWrite count is

        2040 us / 255 = 8.000 us exactly

    which makes every predicted pulse width below an exact multiple of 8 us.

    This is confirmed by the Module 1 captures of the same pin 9 output on
    the Tektronix TDS 1002C-EDU, which does have an automatic pulse width
    measurement:

        analogWrite(51)  = 20.0%  ->  51 * 8 us = 408 us    measured 408.1 us
        analogWrite(204) = 80.0%  -> 204 * 8 us = 1632 us   measured 1.632 ms

    Both agree with 8.000 us per count to within the resolution of the
    measurement, so the predicted widths in section 3 rest on a timebase
    that has already been checked against a counter, not on assumption.


2. DIRECTION SYMMETRY (Serial Monitor, pin 11 toggled at a fixed knob setting)

    The point of this test is that flipping the direction input must swap
    which pin carries PWM without changing the commanded duty. Four knob
    settings were held while pin 11 was toggled:

    ADC      duty        heat (pin 11 = 5V)        cool (pin 11 = 0V)
    -----    ---------   -----------------------   -----------------------
    343.2     85 / 255   pin 9 = PWM, pin 10 = 0V  pin 9 = 0V, pin 10 = PWM
    791.8    197 / 255   pin 9 = PWM, pin 10 = 0V  pin 9 = 0V, pin 10 = PWM
    870.0    216 / 255   pin 9 = PWM, pin 10 = 0V  pin 9 = 0V, pin 10 = PWM
    1020.2   254 / 255   pin 9 = PWM, pin 10 = 0V  pin 9 = 0V, pin 10 = PWM

    In every case the duty field was identical either side of the toggle.
    Exactly one input carried PWM at a time; the other read 0 V, never 5 V.

    Rotation, observed on the motor shaft:

        HEAT / clockwise        -> shaft turned clockwise
        COOL / counterclockwise -> shaft turned counterclockwise

    so the sketch's labels match the physical sense of rotation and no
    swap of the motor leads at the barrier strip was needed.

4. DOUBLE QUANTIZATION IN THE ADC-TO-PWM CHAIN

    At pot ADC = 269.0 the reported duty alternated between 66 and 67
    between consecutive lines. This is not electrical noise. The sketch
    averages 200 raw reads into a float, then map() takes (long)potAdc,
    so the average is truncated to an integer BEFORE the 10-bit to 8-bit
    scaling:

        floor(268.x) -> 268 -> 268 * 255 / 1023 = 66.8 -> 66
        floor(269.0) -> 269 -> 269 * 255 / 1023 = 67.0 -> 67

    The averaging buys resolution in the printed ADC column, and then the
    integer map() throws most of it away again at the PWM output. There is
    a double quantization in the chain, not one.


5. OSCILLOSCOPE OBSERVATIONS (BK 2120B, Part 3B and 3C)

    This instrument is analog and has no cursor or measurement panel, so
    every value has to come off the graticule. What the traces establish:

    - The active H-bridge input is a clean two-level rectangular wave. On
      the CRT the high plateau and the low plateau appear as two separate
      horizontal lines, because the beam crosses the switching edges far
      faster than it traverses the flat parts and so the edges are much
      dimmer than the levels.

    - The idle input is a single flat line with no switching on it, which
      is the check that matters: only one side of the bridge is ever being
      driven.

    - Turning the knob down moved the trace from mostly-high with short
      low gaps to mostly-low with short high pulses. The high fraction of
      the period tracked the commanded duty. Reading duty off this scope
      does not require the TIME/DIV setting at all, since

          duty = (high divisions) / (period divisions)

      is a ratio and the sweep rate cancels.

    - Photographs of the screen were taken with the front panel in frame,
      because on an analog scope the trace carries no information without
      the VOLTS/DIV and TIME/DIV settings that produced it. The settings
      are not legible enough in our photographs to quote amplitudes to
      better than about a division, so no voltage figure is claimed here
      from the 2120B. The amplitude verification for the H-bridge inputs
      comes from the Module 1 Tektronix captures of the same pin 9 output
      (5.36 V high, 490.421 Hz, duty settable to 20.0% and 80.0%).


6. SAFETY NOTES

    - Scope ground clip on Arduino GND for every capture. Never on M+ or M-.
    - TEC module and thermal switch unplugged from the load terminals for
      all of Part 3, so the only load was the small DC motor.
    - Both H-bridge inputs are driven LOW in setup() before anything else,
      so the bridge is not commanded during boot or reset.
