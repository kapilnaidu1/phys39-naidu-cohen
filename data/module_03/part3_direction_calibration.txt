=====================================================================
WHY THESE TWO RUNS FIX THE CALIBRATION
=====================================================================

Result

    pin 11 = 0 V  ->  PWM on pin 10  ->  plate rises  ->  pin 10 is HEAT
    pin 11 = 5 V  ->  PWM on pin  9  ->  plate falls  ->  pin  9 is COOL

    therefore PIN9_IS_HEAT = false

The rule used

    Passive physics only ever moves the plate TOWARD room temperature.
    Motion away from ambient, or motion toward ambient much faster than
    passive decay allows, is the Peltier working and nothing else. Motion
    toward ambient at roughly the passive rate proves nothing, because an
    unpowered plate does exactly that.

Run A, the heating direction

    The plate climbed from 30.55 to 31.28 C in a 23 C room. Passive loss
    at that point pulls DOWN at about 8 / 146 = 0.055 C/s, so the drive is
    supplying 0.209 + 0.055 = 0.26 C/s and beating passive loss roughly
    fourfold. Nothing but active heating raises a plate above ambient.

Run B, the cooling direction

    The first window falls at 0.288 C/s against a passive rate of about
    0.057 C/s, a factor of 5.

    The second window is the stronger evidence. At 24.4 C the plate is only
    1.4 C above ambient, so passive decay has almost nothing left to give:
    1.4 / 146 = 0.009 C/s. The measured fall is 0.118 C/s, about 12x
    faster. Extrapolating, the plate crosses 23 C roughly 10 s after this
    window ends.

    That the ratio GROWS as ambient is approached, 5x at 31 C and 12x at
    24 C, is the expected shape: passive loss dies off with the gradient
    while the Peltier keeps pumping at constant duty.

Sensor mounting, settled as a side effect

    The two runs give OPPOSITE signs in the two switch positions. A
    thermistor mounted on the wrong face of the Peltier would have moved
    the same way in both, since that face heats whichever direction the
    current takes relative to the face being controlled. It did not, so
    the sensor reads the controlled face.

Passive time constant used above

    tau = 146 s, from an undriven decay earlier in the session: the plate
    at 9.1 C above ambient fell at 0.0625 C/s, so tau = 9.1 / 0.0625.
    This is the plate on its coolant loop. It is not the 15 s figure from
    the datasheet, which is the bare sensor in air.

What is NOT evidence

    An apparent rise from 23.4 to 32.3 C in about 7 s appears earlier in
    the session logs. Those reports read PWM: 0 and DISARMED, so no drive
    was commanded, and the decay afterwards had a 15 s time constant,
    matching a bare sensor in air. That was a finger on the thermistor.
    It is recorded here so it is not mistaken for a drive run.

Conversion chain cross-check

    Every R field above agrees with the beta model to better than
    0.03 kOhm at both ends of both runs, using Rnominal = 100 kOhm,
    Beta = 4540 K, Rfixed = 100 kOhm. The temperature column is therefore
    not being produced by a mis-set constant.
