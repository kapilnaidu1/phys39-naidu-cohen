Programs: python/tec_control_gui.py driving arduino/module_03/tec_python_control

Ambient:  about 21.5 C
Checked at the bench by the instructor.

This is the run that satisfies Part 7. It supersedes the earlier attempt in
part7_heat_cool_cycle_gui.txt, which was taken at a quarter of the commanded
drive. See the last section.

AS WITH EVERY RUN SO FAR, THE DIRECTION LABELS ARE INVERTED. The board was
carrying a copy of the sketch with PIN9_IS_HEAT = true. The temperature, time
and duty columns are correct.

RESULT

    minimum   -16.00 C   at t = 411.5 s
    maximum   +54.10 C   at t = 450.0 s
    swing      70.10 C

    That is 37.5 C BELOW ambient and 32.6 C ABOVE it. Both extremes are far
    outside anything passive heat flow can produce, since passive physics
    only ever moves the plate toward room temperature. The whole 70 C range
    is the Peltier doing work.

WHAT WAS COMMANDED

    t =   0.5 to  25      duty 0, plate at 26.2 C, left warm by the previous run
    t =  25.5 to 278      "HEAT", duty 48       26.11 -> 19.78 C
    t = 278   to 281      duty 0
    t = 281   to 411.5    "HEAT", duty 96 -> 254 19.07 -> -16.00 C
    t = 411.5 to 450      "COOL", duty about 250 -16.00 -> +54.10 C

    Commanded HEAT, plate fell to -16. Commanded COOL, plate rose to +54.
    Inverted, consistently, in both directions.

RATES

    cooling leg, duty 96 to 254

        t 281.0 -> 320.0     19.07 ->   8.94    -0.260 C/s
        t 320.0 -> 360.0      8.94 ->  -7.19    -0.403 C/s
        t 360.0 -> 411.5     -7.19 -> -16.00    -0.171 C/s
        whole leg                              -0.269 C/s

        The middle interval is the fastest, not the first. Duty was still
        being raised through the early part of the leg, and the last interval
        slows because the plate is approaching the cold limit of the module,
        where the temperature difference across it is largest and it can pump
        least.

    heating leg, duty about 250

        t 411.5 -> 425.0    -16.00 ->  15.17    +2.309 C/s
        t 425.0 -> 440.0     15.17 ->  42.21    +1.803 C/s
        t 440.0 -> 450.0     42.21 ->  54.10    +1.189 C/s
        whole leg                               +1.821 C/s

    HEATING IS 6.8 TIMES FASTER THAN COOLING, +1.82 against -0.269 C/s.

    This is the expected Peltier asymmetry and this run measures it much
    better than the earlier one did, which showed a factor of 3.7 at partial
    drive. On the heating leg the module delivers pumped heat PLUS its own
    I-squared-R heating. On the cooling leg that same Joule heating opposes
    the pumping, and it grows as the square of the current while the pumping
    grows only linearly. So the asymmetry gets WORSE at higher drive, which
    is why the factor rose from 3.7 to 6.8 when duty went from 159 to 250.

    That is the practical argument for not running a Peltier cooler flat out.
    Past a certain current the extra Joule heating costs more than the extra
    pumping gains, and the cold side stops improving.

SENSOR RANGE AT THE COLD END

    At -16 C the thermistor is about 1.13 MOhm, putting the divider at 4.60 V
    and ADC 940. That is inside the sketch's plausibility window of 20 to
    1000, so the reading is valid, but it is close to the top.

    Sensitivity there is about 25.6 mV/K, so one ADC count is 0.19 C, against
    0.079 C per count at room temperature. The cold end of this run is about
    2.4x coarser than the middle of the range. Still well inside what the
    measurement needs, but worth knowing that the -16.00 figure carries more
    quantization uncertainty than the +54.10 figure does.

WHY THE EARLIER PART 7 RUN LOOKED LIKE A FAULT

    The previous attempt reached only +27 C and -10 C from ambient and was
    read at the bench as a hardware problem: a sagging supply, or a heat
    exchanger that was not circulating.

    It was neither. The sketch had maxDuty = 64, and applyDrive() clamps the
    commanded duty to it:

        int duty = constrain(commandedPwm, 0, maxDuty);

    The GUI said 255. The bridge received 64. The plate was giving an honest
    response to a quarter of the commanded drive, and the curve was correct
    in every respect except that the number on the screen was not the number
    being delivered.

    Two things were announced and neither was noticed: the boot banner
    printed the ceiling, and every acknowledgement above it printed CAPPED.
    Both went to the terminal. Nobody reads a banner they have seen twenty
    times.

    THE LESSON, which is the one worth writing down. A safety limit that the
    operator has forgotten about does not present itself as a safety limit.
    It presents as a broken instrument, and it sends you looking at the
    supply, the wiring and the heat exchanger, none of which had anything
    wrong with them. An output limit is only safe if it is impossible to
    forget, which means it belongs where the operator is already looking.

    Fixed two ways. maxDuty is now 255, so commanded and delivered duty are
    the same number. And the GUI now puts CAPPED and OUTPUT CEILING on the
    window in orange rather than only in the terminal, so if a ceiling is
    ever set again it contradicts the PWM readout right next to it.

    Safety now rests on the thermal switch in series with the module, on the
    temperature channel, and on not leaving a full-drive heating run
    unattended. At +1.82 C/s that last one is not optional: this plate covers
    70 C in under 40 seconds.
