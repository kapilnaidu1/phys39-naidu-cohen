    The plate bottomed at -6.79 C and then warmed steadily, at a rate that
    was still growing when the run ended, with the cooling command unchanged
    at duty 251 the whole time.

    WHAT THIS IS. Not a code fault and not a command that stopped being
    sent. The duty column shows 251 on every row. It is the module reaching
    its temperature-difference limit and then losing ground.

    A Peltier pumps heat from its cold face to its hot face, and the amount
    it can pump falls as the difference between the two faces grows. Its own
    Joule heating is dumped into the hot face as well, and that grows as the
    square of the current. So if the hot side is not being cleared fast
    enough, the hot face climbs, the difference across the module widens, the
    pumping capacity collapses, and the cold face comes back up. That is
    exactly the shape above: a smooth deceleration to a floor, then reversal.

    WHAT IT SAYS ABOUT THE BENCH. This is direct evidence bearing on the
    Part 1 checklist row "heat exchanger connected to 12 V and operating",
    which is still unrecorded. The -6.79 C floor and the reversal after it
    are what a TEC does when hot-side heat removal is the limiting factor.
    Worth confirming the pump is actually circulating, not just that the fans
    are turning, before drawing any conclusion about the module itself.

    It also sets an honest limit on what this instrument can claim. It will
    not hold a setpoint below roughly -7 C in this configuration, not because
    of anything in the control code, but because the hardware runs out of
    pumping capacity there.

COMPARISON WITH THE EARLIER FULL-DRIVE RUN

    cold and was driven colder; this one began at 52 C and had to remove the

HEATING AND COOLING RATES

    heating  +0.913 C/s      from 26.75 C, duty ramped 8 -> 255
    cooling  -1.298 C/s      first 10 s, from 52.23 C, duty 251

    The cooling figure LOOKS faster here, which is the opposite of the
    asymmetry measured elsewhere, and it is an artifact rather than a result.
    The cooling leg started at 52 C, about 30 C above ambient, where passive
    loss to the room contributes strongly in the same direction. The heating
    leg started near ambient, where passive loss opposes it, and spent part
    of its interval at a duty still ramping up from 8.

    The clean asymmetry measurement is in part7_full_drive_70C_swing.txt,
    where both legs were driven at steady full duty: +1.821 against
    -0.269 C/s, a factor of 6.8. Rates taken far from ambient with a ramping
    duty are not comparable to rates taken at steady drive, and are recorded
    here only so the raw numbers are not mistaken for a contradiction.
