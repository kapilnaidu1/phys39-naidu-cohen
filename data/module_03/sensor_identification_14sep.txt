Phys 39 Module 3. The 83 C that was not a temperature.
Naidu / Cohen. 14 September 2026, TEC power OFF throughout.

RESOLVED: the thermistor was not connected. A0 was floating.


WHAT HAPPENED

Running tec_manual_fixed_direction with the Module 3 wiring in progress, the
sketch reported about 67 C rising to 83 C, at PWM 0, with no current in the
TEC. Printing the raw chain gave:

  Temperature (C): 82.64, Time (s): 61.00, PWM: 0, Active PWM pin: none
      [ADC = 80.0, V = 0.391, R = 8.48 kOhm, pot ADC = 0]

steady to one ADC count over many consecutive reports.

The thermistor had not been wired to A0 yet.


WHY THIS IS WORTH RECORDING RATHER THAN DELETING

An unconnected analog input does not read zero and does not look broken. It
returned a rock-steady 80.0 after 500-sample averaging, which is exactly the
signature we would accept as a good measurement from a real sensor. The
averaging, which is there to improve precision, made the artefact look more
trustworthy than a noisy one would have.

Everything downstream was arithmetic performed faithfully on nothing:
0.391 V became 8.48 kOhm through the divider inversion, and 8.48 kOhm became
82.64 C through the beta model. Neither step was wrong. The chain was
correct and the input was meaningless.

Two false diagnoses were produced before the connection was checked:

  1. That the TEC plate carried a 10 kOhm sensor rather than the 100 kOhm
     part from Module 2, since 8.48 kOhm is about 29 C for a 10 kOhm NTC.

  2. That the divider was inverted, with the 100 kOhm thermistor in the
     upper leg over a 10 kOhm lower leg, since that also gives exactly
     0.391 V and puts the sensor at 21.8 C, room temperature.

Both were self-consistent. Both were arithmetic on a floating pin.


THE SAME MISTAKE TWICE IN THIS COURSE

Module 2 lost time to a loose thermistor lead while three physical
explanations were proposed and refuted in turn: mains pickup, hand
capacitance, and convection. None of them could have found a bad contact.

Module 3 lost time to an unconnected thermistor while two wiring theories
were proposed. Neither of them could have found an absent sensor.

The pattern is identical. In both cases the reasoning moved to the model
before the connection was verified, and in both cases the model was not the
problem. The order that would have worked, both times:

  1. Is the sensor connected?
  2. Does it respond, with the right sign, when disturbed?
  3. Only then, is the model right?


THE TEN-SECOND TEST THAT CATCHES ALL THREE FAILURES

Warm the thermistor between finger and thumb and watch the ADC field.

  ADC does not move          -> the sensor is not in the circuit
  ADC moves the wrong way    -> the legs are swapped
  ADC falls as it warms      -> correct: NTC as the lower leg

That single test would have caught the Module 2 loose lead, the Module 3
missing connection, and an inverted divider. It costs nothing and it is now
written into the header of all three Module 3 sketches.


EXPECTED VALUES ONCE IT IS WIRED

    5V --- 100 kOhm --- A0 --- thermistor --- GND

  ADC about 560, 2.75 V, about 120 kOhm, about 21 C at room temperature,
  and the ADC must fall when the thermistor is warmed.

If the values come back far from that, diagnose then, with a divider that
actually exists. The two hypotheses above are still available and still
distinguishable by the sign of the response.
