Phys 39 Module 3. Identifying the thermistor channel on A0.
Naidu / Cohen. 14 September 2026, TEC power OFF throughout.

STATUS: UNRESOLVED. Two candidate wirings fit the measurement equally well.
The test that separates them is at the end of this file and takes 20 seconds.


THE SYMPTOM

Running tec_manual_fixed_direction with the Module 3 wiring, the sketch
reported about 67 C rising to 83 C, at PWM 0, with the heat exchanger
running and no current in the TEC.

Two things showed immediately that this was not a temperature.

  1. It rose about 15 C in a minute with the actuator unpowered. An
     unpowered TEC does not drive its plate in any direction.

  2. Consecutive reports repeated the identical value 82.64, then stepped to
     82.27 and back. Real thermal drift does not quantize. A value that
     repeats and then moves in fixed jumps is an ADC running out of
     resolution, not a plate changing temperature.


THE RAW MEASUREMENT

Printing the raw chain gave the number directly instead of by inference:

  Temperature (C): 82.64, Time (s): 61.00, PWM: 0, Active PWM pin: none
      [ADC = 80.0, V = 0.391, R = 8.48 kOhm, pot ADC = 0]

Steady to one ADC count over many consecutive reports. That steadiness is
itself evidence: the intermittent contact that bit us in Module 2 could
never hold a single count. So 0.391 V at the A0 node is a real, stable
measurement. The question is only what is on either side of that node.


THE AMBIGUITY

The sketch computes resistance assuming the thermistor is the LOWER leg:

    Rth = Rfixed * V / (Vref - V)

That equation is only true if the thermistor really is the lower leg. Drop
that assumption and 0.391 V has two explanations, and both fit.

  CASE A. A 10 kOhm NTC as the lower leg, 100 kOhm in the upper leg.
          Lower leg = 100k * 0.391 / (5 - 0.391) = 8.48 kOhm.
          For a 10 kOhm part that is about 29 C.
          Implies the plate sensor is a different part from Module 2.

  CASE B. The Module 2 100 kOhm NTC as the UPPER leg, with a 10 kOhm
          resistor as the lower leg, so A0 reads across the RESISTOR and
          not across the sensor. Then
              V = Vref * Rlow / (Rlow + Rth)
              Rth = 10k * (5 / 0.391 - 1) = 117.9 kOhm
          For the 100 kOhm TDK part that is 21.8 C, which is just room
          temperature. Nothing is wrong with the sensor at all; the divider
          is upside down and the resistor is a tenth of the intended value.

Both give exactly ADC 80. One measurement at one temperature cannot choose
between them, which is the real lesson here: a single number read through an
assumed model cannot test that model.

Recorded honestly: the first pass through this concluded case A and said so.
That was too quick. Case B was not considered, and it is the more mundane
explanation, which usually means the more likely one.


THE TEST THAT SEPARATES THEM

Warm the thermistor between finger and thumb and watch the ADC field in the
bracket. An NTC always falls in resistance when warmed. Which leg it sits in
decides which way that moves the divider.

  Case A, thermistor is the lower leg:
      warming lowers the lower leg -> V falls -> ADC FALLS
      predicted about 80 -> 72 for a 3 K rise

  Case B, thermistor is the upper leg:
      warming lowers the upper leg -> V rises -> ADC RISES
      predicted about 80 -> 92 for a 3 K rise

Read the SIGN, not the size. Both are a few counts per degree.

This is exactly the orientation check from Module 2 Part 1, which is there
precisely because the sign of the response is what tells you the sensor is
in the leg you think it is in.

Second, independent check: read the colour bands on the resistor.
    100 kOhm = brown, black, yellow
     10 kOhm = brown, black, orange
They differ by one band. Or measure the thermistor directly with a
multimeter, power off and one lead lifted: about 120 kOhm means the Module 2
part, about 12 kOhm means a 10 kOhm part.


WHAT TO SET AFTERWARDS

  Case A confirmed:  SENSOR_IS_10K 1   Rfixed = 100000.0
                     and get the real B value from the part number, since
                     3435 K was an assumption
  Case B confirmed:  rewire so the thermistor sits between A0 and GND, as
                     the assignment specifies, then SENSOR_IS_10K 0 and
                     Rfixed = the measured resistor value

Either way the divider should end up with both legs comparable. At ADC 80
one count is worth 0.361 C, which is exactly the 82.64 to 82.27 step in the
capture above. Near mid scale one count is worth about 0.1 C. A divider is
most sensitive when the two legs are similar in value, which is why Module 2
sat near ADC 512 with 100 kOhm against 100 kOhm.


WHY THIS IS A SAFETY ITEM AND NOT JUST AN ACCURACY ONE

The reported temperature is the primary indication that the TEC plate is not
running away. The thermal switch is a backstop, not a gauge. Applying
current while the temperature channel is misconfigured means losing the
instrument that would show a problem developing, and in case B the sign of
the response is inverted, so a heating plate would read as cooling.
