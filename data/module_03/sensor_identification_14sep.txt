Phys 39 Module 3. Identifying the TEC plate thermistor.
Naidu / Cohen. 14 September 2026, TEC power OFF throughout.

THE SYMPTOM

On first running tec_manual_fixed_direction with the TEC plate thermistor on
A0, the sketch reported temperatures around 67 C rising to 83 C, at PWM 0,
with the heat exchanger running and no current in the TEC.

Two things showed immediately that this was not a temperature.

  1. It rose about 15 C in a minute with the actuator unpowered. An
     unpowered TEC does not drive its plate in any direction.

  2. Consecutive reports repeated the identical value, 82.64, then stepped
     to 82.27 and back. Real thermal drift does not quantize. A repeated
     value that steps in fixed jumps is the signature of an ADC operating
     with almost no resolution left.

THE RAW CHAIN

Adding the raw fields to the serial line gave the measurement directly,
rather than by inference:

  Temperature (C): 82.64, Time (s): 61.00, PWM: 0, Active PWM pin: none
      [ADC = 80.0, V = 0.391, R = 8.48 kOhm, pot ADC = 0]

Steady to one ADC count over many reports. That steadiness matters: an
intermittent connection, which is what bit us in Module 2, could not hold a
single count. The 8.48 kOhm is a real resistance.

WHAT 8.48 kOhm MEANS

Read through candidate sensor models:

  model                          resistance 8.48 kOhm implies
  ----------------------------   ----------------------------
  100 kOhm, B = 4540  (Module 2)            82.7 C
   10 kOhm, B = 3435                        29.3 C
   10 kOhm, B = 3950                        28.8 C
   10 kOhm, B = 3892                        28.8 C

The plate was at room temperature to the touch, so the 100 kOhm model is
excluded and the sensor is a 10 kOhm part.

Worth noting for the write-up: the three 10 kOhm candidates agree to within
half a degree here. That is not luck. All beta models are pinned at the
25 C anchor, so near 25 C the choice of B barely matters. The exact B value
only becomes important far from the anchor, which is the same point Module 2
made about extrapolating B25/100 down to 15 C.

WHY THE UPPER LEG SHOULD ALSO CHANGE

Getting Rnominal and Beta right fixes the reported number but not the
resolution. The divider is still badly out of balance:

  upper leg    operating point    sensitivity    resolution
  ---------    ---------------    -----------    ----------------
  100 kOhm     ADC  80, 0.391 V   13.5 mV/K      0.361 C per count
   10 kOhm     ADC 469, 2.294 V   46.6 mV/K      0.105 C per count

The 0.361 C figure is exactly the 82.64 to 82.27 step we observed, which
confirms the model of what is happening.

A divider is most sensitive when the two legs are comparable, which is why
Module 2 sat near ADC 512 with 100 kOhm against 100 kOhm. Putting a 10 kOhm
resistor in the upper leg restores that condition and buys a factor of 3.4
in temperature resolution for the price of one resistor.

ACTION TAKEN IN THE CODE

All three Module 3 sketches now carry a single SENSOR_IS_10K switch that
selects Rnominal, Beta and a printed sensor name, plus an Rfixed constant
with a comment stating that it is physical and must match the resistor
actually installed. Each sketch prints the active sensor profile at startup,
so every serial log says which calibration produced it.
