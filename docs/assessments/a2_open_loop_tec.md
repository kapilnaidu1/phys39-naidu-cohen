## 3. The derivation *(3 pts, the heaviest item)*

Three things, all required:

**(a) PWM average-current proof.** From the definitions of the averages over
one period, with current I for D*tau and zero otherwise, show
`<I> = D I` and `<I^2> = D I^2`. Explain why `<I^2>` is not `<I>^2`, and
connect it to the measured susceptibility: Peltier follows `<I>` and Joule
follows `<I^2>`, both linear in D under PWM, so the susceptibility is
approximately constant and the graph roughly straight.

**(b) Steady-state slope derivation.** From `C dT/dt = Q_TEC - G(T - T0)` at
`dT/dt = 0`, with `Q_TEC = d Qp + |d| Qj`, obtain

```
dTh/dd = (Qp + Qj)/G        dTc/dd = (Qp - Qj)/G
```

and hence, the 1/255 cancelling from the ratio,

```
Qj/Qp = (r - 1)/(r + 1)
```

**(c) Numerical result.** Evaluate `Qj/Qp` from the measured r. Check: r = 2
gives exactly 1/3.

Full working is in section 5 of the module note.

## 6. Passive conduction *(part of 2 pts with item 5)*

Hotter than the room, heat flows out. Colder, heat flows in. Either way it
drives the plate back toward room temperature, opposing heating and cooling
alike. Because that opposition is approximately symmetric it enters both
branches through the same G and **cannot by itself produce unequal slopes**.
The asymmetry is Joule heating, which adds on the heating branch and
subtracts on the cooling branch.
