## 1. Question

What are the thermal conductivity k of the 6061-T6 rod and the side
heat-transfer coefficient h to the room air, and does the one-dimensional
heat equation with those values reproduce the measured T(x, t)?

## 2. Apparatus

A TEC under PI control (Arduino Uno, H-bridge, cooling commands multiplied by
r = 2.771 from the Module 4 susceptibilities) drives the TEC plate as
40 ± 16 °C sinusoids with periods 100 to 480 s. The rod (D = 12.757 mm,
L = 281.9 mm from the first hole to the tip) carries thermistors T1 to T5 at
0, 25.4, 50.8, 76.2 and 101.6 mm. T1, in the first hole, is the measured
boundary at x = 0. The Python GUI records all six thermistors every 0.5 s
(raw files in `data/module_08/` and `data/module_08_sammy/`).

## 3. Model

Energy balance on a slice plus Fourier's law and Newton cooling:

∂θ/∂t = κ ∂²θ/∂x² − νθ, θ = T − T∞, κ = k/(ρc), ν = hP/(ρcA).

For a periodic base θ(0, t) = B₀ cos ωt, a settled solution on a semi-infinite
rod is B₀ e^(−qx) cos(ωt − q′x), with q² − q′² = ν/κ and 2qq′ = ω/κ. So

κ = ω/(2qq′), ν = κ(q² − q′²), k = κρc, h = νρcA/P = νρcR/2.

Units: ω/(qq′) is s⁻¹/m⁻² = m²/s; κρc is m²/s · kg/m³ · J/(kg K) = W/(m K);
νρcR is s⁻¹ · kg/m³ · J/(kg K) · m = W/(m² K).

The primary fits use the finite rod with an insulated tip,
θ ∝ cosh(s(L − x))/cosh(sL), s = q + iq′, fitted jointly to ln B and the
unwrapped phase of T1 to T5.

## 5. Parameter table

Generated by `python/analysis/part2_parameters.py`
(`data/module_08/part2_parameter_table.md`, `part2_parameters.csv`, `.txt`).

**Uncertainty method.** Monte Carlo, 2000 draws.

- **Statistical part.** Each window's B and φ errors are the cycle-to-cycle
  scatter, scaled up by √(χ²/dof) of the window's fit. This scaling is large
  (2.5 to 10), because the spatial misfit is much bigger than the cycle
  scatter.
- **Common part.** In every draw, T2 to T5 move by an independent 1 mm (sd),
  L by 1.3 mm and D by 0.006 mm. Each draw applies the same shift to all nine
  windows, because the holes and the sensor map were the same on every day.
- **Combining.** The combined value is weighted by the statistical errors.
  Its total error is the spread of that weighted mean over the draws.
- **Caveat.** This probably double counts somewhat: the χ² scaling already
  absorbs scatter that looks like position error. Treat the totals as
  generous.

## 6. k and h, per period and combined

| Period | κ (10⁻⁵ m²/s) | k (W/(m K)) | h (W/(m² K)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 s (2 windows) | 9.30 ± 0.20 | 225 ± 5 | 17 ± 10 |
| 180 s | 9.24 ± 0.37 | 224 ± 9 | 9 ± 11 |
| 240 s (2) | 9.11 ± 0.17 | 220 ± 4 | 17.0 ± 3.9 |
| 300 s | 9.27 ± 0.29 | 224 ± 7 | 12.5 ± 4.8 |
| 480 s (3) | 9.06 ± 0.20 | 219 ± 5 | 16.1 ± 2.4 |
| all nine | 9.17 ± 0.18 | 222 ± 4 | 15.6 ± 1.9 |
| six with ≥ 5 cycles | 9.20 ± 0.19 | 222 ± 5 | 15.5 ± 2.1 |

Per-period errors are statistical only. Errors on the combined rows are total.

- **Frequency consistency.** κ and h agree across periods and days (χ² 3.1 and
  1.4 for 8 degrees of freedom). ν is poorly fixed at 100 and 180 s, because
  there q ≈ q′ and ν comes from a small difference.
- **Weighting.** If the 1 mm position error is added in quadrature to each
  window, as in `angstrom_finite_fit.py`, instead of drawn in common, the
  weighted κ is 9.29 rather than 9.17. That 1.3 % is a method systematic.
- **k against the literature.** k = 222 ± 4 W/(m K) is 33 % above the 6061-T6
  value of 167 and about 11σ above the top of the 166 to 172 range. This is
  the main open problem (section 9).
- **h against natural convection.** The rod stands vertically (confirmed by
  Kapil, 11 Nov), so the vertical correlation applies; the horizontal-cylinder
  numbers are kept only for scale. Churchill and Chu correlations for still
  air, with air properties at 300 K from Incropera et al. Table A.4:
  - horizontal cylinder of diameter D: 5.3, 6.2 and 6.7 W/(m² K) for surface
    excess 5, 10 and 15 K;
  - vertical plate of height L: 3 to 4 W/(m² K). This formula is not strictly
    valid here, because the rod is thin enough that curvature raises h.

  For laminar natural convection h ∝ ΔT^(1/4), so a small oscillation sees
  d(hΔT)/dΔT = 1.25 h, about 7 to 8 W/(m² K). Linearised radiation adds
  6.1ε W/(m² K) at 300 K. The rod's emissivity was not measured; bare
  aluminum would make this small.

  The measured 15.6 ± 1.9 is about twice the still-air scale. Possible
  reasons: air moving near the rod from the heat-sink fans or the pump, a
  high emissivity, or ν biased high (section 7).

**m² comparison:**

| Source | m (1/m) | m² (1/m²) |
|---|---|---|
| Module 7 stationary fin, full-length profile | 4.117 ± 0.07 | 16.9 ± 0.6 |
| Module 8 mean profile, a_i fit (per window; 10 Nov without T2) | 3.79 to 4.61 | about 18 |
| Angstrom ν/κ, 9 windows | 4.70 ± 0.29 | 22.1 ± 2.7 |

The Angstrom value sits about 2σ above Module 7. A 25 % higher incremental
coefficient for the oscillation (see above) would predict an m ratio of
√1.25 = 1.12. The observed ratio is 4.70/4.12 = 1.14. This fits, but it is
only a hypothesis; the ν error is too large to test it.

## 7. Model against data

`python/analysis/angstrom_model_compare.py` solves the rod equation by
Crank-Nicolson:

- dx = 1 mm and dt = 0.5 s, with an insulated tip;
- κ = 9.17 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s and ν = 2.02 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, fixed with no fitting;
- driven by the measured T1(t) over the whole run, starting from the measured
  profile.

Against the analytic finite-rod solution the solver agrees to 10⁻⁴ in
amplitude and phase. Figures: `docs/figures/module_08/model_compare_*.png`.
Numbers: `data/module_08/model_compare_*.txt`.

In the retained windows, measured over modelled, for T3 to T5 only (T2 is
discussed separately below):

| Window | amplitude ratio | phase meas − model (rad) | mean residual (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Nov 100 s | 1.01 to 1.03 | +0.05 to +0.07 | +0.28 to +0.53 |
| 2 Nov 480 s | 1.01 to 1.02 | 0.00 to +0.01 | +0.47 to +0.63 |
| 4 Nov 180 s | 0.99 to 1.01 | −0.01 to +0.01 | +0.19 to +0.33 |
| 4 Nov 240 s | 1.00 to 1.02 | 0.00 to +0.01 | +0.28 to +0.44 |
| 4 Nov 300 s | 1.02 | −0.01 to 0.00 | +0.54 to +0.78 |
| 10 Nov 480 s | 1.01 | −0.02 to 0.00 | +0.15 to +0.33 |
| 10 Nov 240 s | 0.99 to 1.01 | −0.02 to 0.00 | +0.02 to +0.14 |
| 10 Nov 100 s | 0.99 to 1.01 | −0.01 to +0.04 | +0.04 to +0.17 |
| 11 Nov 480 s | 1.00 to 1.02 | −0.01 to +0.01 | +0.38 to +0.44 |

- **The oscillation is reproduced** to about 2 % in amplitude and 0.02 rad in
  phase, with no fitting, on four days and five periods.
- **The mean is not.** The measured rod is 0.02 to 0.8 °C warmer than the model
  at T3 to T5, and the gap grows through a run and persists after the drive
  stops. Rerunning the 4 Nov 240 s case with ν = 1.70 × 10⁻³ or 1.55 × 10⁻³
  instead of 2.02 × 10⁻³ cuts the mean residual at T5 from +0.44 to +0.14 or
  −0.01 °C, while the amplitudes change by about 1 %. So the oscillation fixes
  ν only loosely, and the mean profile prefers a smaller ν. That is the m²
  comparison above seen from the time domain.
- **T2 is high on every day,** not only 10 and 11 November. Its amplitude is
  2 to 7 % above the model and its phase leads by 0.01 to 0.09 rad. Its mean
  is +1.3 °C high on 10 Nov and +0.7 °C on 11 Nov. That looks like T2 sensing
  closer to T1 than 25.4 mm, or better coupled than the others.

## 8. Validity checks with the final parameters

- **mL:** with m = √(ν/κ) = 4.70 m⁻¹, mL = 1.33 ± 0.08. With the Module 7 m
  it is 1.16.
- **Finite length at T5:**
  - **Oscillation:** the semi-infinite and finite-rod amplitudes differ by
    0.12, 0.68, 1.19, 2.00 and 3.97 % at 100, 180, 240, 300 and 480 s. This is
    why the finite-rod fit is primary.
  - **Mean profile:** the semi-infinite form is 9.5 % off at T5, so only the
    cosh form is used.
- **Transverse Biot number:** Bi = hR/(2k) = (ν/κ)R²/4 = 2.3 × 10⁻⁴, far below
  0.1. One temperature per cross section is fine.
- **Conclusion:** the one-dimensional finite-rod model holds for T1 to T5 at
  all five periods.

## 9. Systematic errors

1. **κ, and therefore k, is 33 % above the handbook value.**
   - A common scale error in spacing scales κ as (spacing)² but leaves ν
     unchanged. Explaining 33 % would need 22 mm spacing instead of 25.4 mm,
     and the calipers read 25.4 mm on 10 Nov.
   - Independent 1 mm position errors are already in the Monte Carlo.
   - Possible remaining causes:
     - The bead's effective position may differ from the hole centre. The
       direction depends on which sensors are off.
     - The rod may not be 6061-T6. If it is a higher-conductivity alloy, κ
       would be higher; this is unverified.
     - ρ and c are assumed, not measured. k inherits their error, but κ does
       not.
2. **T2 anomaly** (section 7). It raises T2's amplitude and advances its phase,
   which lowers the fitted q and q′ near the base. Since κ ∝ 1/(qq′), it biases
   κ upward. The size of this bias is not yet quantified; refitting without T2
   is the next check.
3. **The measured mean is warmer than the model.** Possible causes: heat
   reaching the rod other than through T1 (for example warm air from the TEC
   heat sink rising along the rod), h varying along the rod, or ν from the
   oscillation not applying to the mean (h ∝ ΔT^(1/4)). This biases a single-h
   description. It barely affects κ.
4. **Room temperature and drift.** T∞ enters only the mean profile. ±0.1 °C
   moves the mean-profile m by ±0.03 m⁻¹. q and q′ do not depend on T∞.
5. **Too few settled cycles.** Two windows have 3 cycles. Dropping them changes
   κ from 9.17 to 9.20 (0.3 %).
6. **Base waveform.** The second harmonic is about 2 % of the fundamental.
   The Angstrom fits use the fundamental only, and the model comparison uses
   the measured T1 directly, so there's no bias in the comparison.
7. **Insulated-tip assumption.** The real tip loses some heat. This only
   matters at 480 s, where the whole finite-length correction is 4 % at T5.
8. **Phase unwrapping.** Unwrapped from T1 upward. The largest lag is
   1.86 rad at 100 s, so there's no ambiguity.
